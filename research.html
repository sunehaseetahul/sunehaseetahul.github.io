Research

My research asks how social structures are related to unequal outcomes for women and other disadvantaged groups, and the role policy can play. I use applied microeconometric methods, linking survey, administrative and geospatial data across low-, middle- and high-income countries, and I build research tools, such as indices and dashboards, targeted at policymakers.

Work and labour markets

How can we measure gender inequality in the labour market so that policy can act on it?

Australia, South-East Asia, India, Colombia

I am the academic and quantitative lead of the Gender Equality @ Work Index, which tracks gender equality in Australian work across seven dimensions: participation, pay, hours, stratification, segmentation, security and safety. The Index was launched at Parliament House in 2025, is used to evaluate the Australian Government's Building Women's Careers program, and is cited by the Office for Women. I have since extended it into a family of tools: state-level indices, an industry-level index, and an organisational index that provides the research framework, dashboard and staff and student surveys for the University of Sydney's Gender Equality Plan.

This theme also covers how pay is set, job quality, and how generative AI is reshaping work for older workers and for women.

Health, nutrition and wellbeing

How do gender inequality, income inequality and living conditions shape nutrition and health?

India, Mexico, China, the Pacific Islands, Sierra Leone, Australia

This theme examines the social determinants of malnutrition and obesity in middle-income and Pacific settings. It showed that gender inequality and local income inequality were linked to excess weight, that excess weight carried consequences in the labour market, and that water insecurity was associated with higher purchases of sugary drinks in the Pacific. The work now extends to food and nutrition security in Sierra Leone, and to the health, safety and working conditions of women in Australian workplaces, including nurses and midwives and retail workers.

Gender norms and behaviour

How do policy design, social identity and environmental shocks shape attitudes, preferences and choices?

31 African countries, 25 high-income countries, India

This theme treats gender norms as outcomes that respond to institutions and events. In India, skewed sex ratios were associated with weaker son preference over time, and social identity shaped cognition and labour mobility. Across high-income countries, the share of parental leave reserved for fathers, rather than its overall generosity, was associated with more egalitarian norms, and across Africa, droughts and floods shifted gender attitudes.

Social protection and development

How can social protection programs reach the people who need them, and help them withstand shocks?

Sierra Leone, Indonesia

As an economist and social protection specialist with the World Bank from 2021 to 2026, I have worked on cash transfers, public works and economic inclusion programs in Sierra Leone and Indonesia. This includes how programs can build climate resilience, include people with disability, and target households facing food insecurity, as well as the reach of COVID-19 emergency cash transfers to informal sector workers.
