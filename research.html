Research

My research asks how social structures translate into unequal outcomes for women and disadvantaged groups, and what policy can do about it. I use applied microeconometrics and an interdisciplinary approach, linking survey, administrative and geospatial data.

Theme one

Health, nutrition and wellbeing

How do gender inequality, income inequality and living conditions shape nutrition and health?

India, Mexico, China, the Pacific Islands, Sierra Leone, Australia

This strand examined the social determinants of malnutrition and obesity in middle-income and Pacific settings. It showed that gender inequality and local income inequality were linked to excess weight, and that excess weight carried consequences in the labour market. The work now extends to food and nutrition security in Sierra Leone, and to the health, safety and working conditions of women in Australian workplaces.

Theme two

Gender norms and behaviour

How do policy design, social identity and environmental shocks shape attitudes, preferences and choices?

31 African countries, 25 high-income countries, India

This strand treats gender norms as outcomes that respond to institutions and events. Recent work found that the share of parental leave reserved for fathers, rather than its overall generosity, was associated with more egalitarian norms across high-income countries, and that droughts and floods shifted gender attitudes across Africa.

Theme three

Measuring gender equality at work

How can we measure gender inequality in the labour market so that policy can act on it?

Australia, South-East Asia, Colombia

I lead the quantitative development of the Gender Equality @ Work Index, which tracks gender equality in Australian work across seven dimensions: participation, pay, hours, stratification, segmentation, security and safety. The index was launched at Parliament House in 2025 and is used in government evaluation. State-level, industry and organisational versions are in development, as is comparative work with partners in Austria and Japan.

Theme four

Social protection and development

How can social protection programs reach the people who need them, and help them withstand shocks?

Sierra Leone, Indonesia

As a World Bank economist and social protection specialist, I worked on cash transfers, public works and economic inclusion programs, with a focus on Sierra Leone and Indonesia. This included how programs can build climate resilience, include people with disability, and target households facing food insecurity.
